📄 The published paper behind this data

medi-104-e45970.pdf — the peer-reviewed write-up of this exact study; medi-104-e45970-s001.xlsx — its photographed master chart of all 34 procured packs.

Philips et al., Medicine 2025;104:46(e45970) — “The Citizens Protein Project 2”

India's first publicly crowd-funded study of whey supplements (MESH, Rajagiri Hospital; funded via meshindia.org donations): 34 whey powders — 18 medical-pharmaceutical vs 16 nutraceutical — tested for protein, amino acids, sugars, heavy metals, aflatoxins, hormones, pesticides and undeclared additives.

  • Nutraceuticals averaged 75.6 ± 7.3 g protein/100 g with mean label bias of only −2.7%; pharmaceuticals averaged 29.1 ± 15.4 g, with 83% mislabelled beyond ±5%.
  • Six nutraceutical powders met the “very-high-quality” bar (≥40 g EAA + ≥7 g leucine per 100 g) — no pharmaceutical product came close.
  • Cost per gram of actual protein: ₹2.19–8.79 (nutra) vs ₹6.71–24.77 (pharma) — pharma premiums are disconnected from protein delivered.
  • Taurine-based nitrogen spiking signals in 89% of pharma vs 12.5% of nutra products (S2 at 5.93 and the excluded isolate S29 at 87.1 mg/100 g were the two nutra detections).
  • Aflatoxin in 2 pharma powders (B-Protein, D-Protin); progesterone in 2 pharma + 1 nutra (Naturaltein — the isolate-only S21 excluded above).

Bottom line of the paper, in one sentence: nutraceutical whey concentrates/blends deliver more protein, better amino profiles and cleaner labels per rupee than “doctor-recommended” pharmaceutical powders — which is exactly what this page's product ranking shows.
